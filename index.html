The opportunity

A world deciding on thin evidence.

The most credible measurements of AI's labour-market effects rely on high-frequency payroll data that exists in very few countries, and most of the institutions producing that evidence sit in the same places.

Decisions without local data

Governments, employers and communities are setting labour policy, education, social protection and industrial strategy without enough evidence from their own economies. The social sciences, best placed to explain what AI does to workers and institutions, remain under-resourced relative to the pace of the technology.

  • Asia
  • Africa
  • Middle East
  • Latin America
Four lanes are open

Four pillars, one shared foundation.

Each lane is a place where partners can build something that lasts, and each one holds up the others.

Evidence

Measurement and evidence

Adapting rigorous, high-frequency methods to labour markets unlike those most existing studies were built on, and to the education and public systems that feed them.

Policy

Policy translation

Turning comparative findings into frameworks that governments and institutions outside the usual research centres can act on.

People

Talent and capacity

Growing a generation of researchers and practitioners equipped to study AI's economic effects in their own contexts.

Exchange

Convening and exchange

Creating recurring, evidence-first venues where findings across regions are compared rather than produced in isolation.

The Next Wave

A validated methodology for measuring AI's effects on employment, occupations and wages in economies where such measurement does not yet exist.

Step one: validate

Test it where rigour is possible

Two Global Majority economies with the state capacity and data quality to stress-test the method.

  • Qatar
  • Singapore
Step two: extend

Carry it to the largest economies

Designed to extend to large economies, with partners who bring local data and context.

  • Brazil
  • Nigeria
  • Indonesia
  • Egypt

Published openly. Any country can apply the method to its own data, on its own terms. It is one example of work that needs many more hands.

About Qatar Foundation

Human potential is the most valuable resource.

Qatar Foundation is a not-for-profit organisation advancing education, research and innovation, and community development for the benefit of Qatar and the world. Over three decades, its vision has grown into a multidisciplinary ecosystem of knowledge that fosters lifelong learning, drives innovation and entrepreneurship, and empowers people to drive positive change.
